## Advanced learning stages

### 1. Consultation & cutting plan

This stage focuses on consultation & cutting plan. In modern barbering, technique should follow diagnosis rather than habit. The key principle here is to agree length, movement and finish before cutting.

Section size, distribution, elevation, finger angle, tension and body position must support the intended shape. If one variable changes unintentionally, the same cutting line can produce a different result.

Precision cutting begins with a reproducible plan.

Use consultation, observation and controlled execution together. Think about the client's hair, head/face shape, routine, desired finish and maintenance. Make one deliberate decision at a time, then cross-check the result before making the next adjustment.

For CPD purposes, do more than remember a technique. Explain why it is suitable, recognise when it is not suitable, and be able to adapt it. That is the difference between copying a method and developing professional judgement.


**Professional evidence:** Professional application: using a real, recent or realistic case involving consultation & cutting plan, identify the evidence you would gather, the main risk of making the wrong decision, the option you would choose, one credible alternative you would reject, and justify both decisions in at least 120 words.

### 2. Sectioning and body position

This stage focuses on sectioning and body position. In modern barbering, technique should follow diagnosis rather than habit. The key principle here is to clean sections create repeatable control.

Clean sections expose the relationship between the guide and the hair being cut. Over-wide sections, poor body position or inconsistent tension can hide over-direction and produce unintended weight.

Sectioning is a control system.

### 3. Elevation and graduation

This stage focuses on elevation and graduation. In modern barbering, technique should follow diagnosis rather than habit. The key principle here is to elevation changes weight distribution and shape.

Lower elevation generally retains more weight; increased elevation removes more. The professional decision is not 'which angle is best' but which weight distribution supports the head shape, texture and finish.

Elevation controls where weight is retained.

### 4. Layering and over-direction

This stage focuses on layering and over-direction. In modern barbering, technique should follow diagnosis rather than habit. The key principle here is to over-direction preserves or removes length in predictable areas.

Moving hair away from its natural fall can preserve length in one area and build movement or weight in another. It must be intentional because accidental over-direction creates imbalance.

Over-direction changes length without changing the guide.

### 5. Scissor-over-comb & weight control

This stage focuses on scissor-over-comb & weight control. In modern barbering, technique should follow diagnosis rather than habit. The key principle here is to scissor-over-comb should follow the intended silhouette rather than flatten it.

Comb angle, speed, blade position and visual control determine the plane being created. Work in small zones and repeatedly step back to protect the overall silhouette.

Scissor-over-comb is shape construction, not random refinement.

### 6. Texture, movement & personalisation

This stage focuses on texture, movement & personalisation. In modern barbering, technique should follow diagnosis rather than habit. The key principle here is to point cutting, slicing and texturising must be chosen for a reason.

Point cutting, slicing or controlled texturising can alter movement and visual density, but using them to hide a weak structure usually creates inconsistency and makes future maintenance harder.

Texture should be personalised after structure is secure.

Outlines must belong to the haircut.

### 8. Corrective cutting & quality control

This stage focuses on corrective cutting & quality control. In modern barbering, technique should follow diagnosis rather than habit. The key principle here is to cross-check balance before making large corrective cuts.

Find the structural cause before removing more hair. Compare elevation, over-direction, tension and guide consistency before deciding that additional length must be removed.

Corrective cutting requires restraint.

### 9. Activity, reflection & final assessment

Professional CPD is strongest when knowledge changes what you do. Review the earlier stages and identify the decisions you will carry into real client work.

Useful CPD links an observed result to a technical cause and a planned adjustment.

Reflection should name the variable you would change next time.

Your final assessment checks understanding across the course. The reflection is your evidence of application: be specific about what you will change, how you will measure it and what further development you may need.


**Professional evidence:** Professional application: using a real, recent or realistic case involving activity, reflection & final assessment, identify the evidence you would gather, the main risk of making the wrong decision, the option you would choose, one credible alternative you would reject, and justify both decisions in at least 120 words.
