## Advanced learning stages

### 1. Positioning, target client & service menu

This stage focuses on positioning, target client & service menu. In modern barbering, technique should follow diagnosis rather than habit. The key principle here is to choose a clear client promise instead of trying to be everything to everyone.

Do not price only by competitors. Understand service time, direct cost, overhead contribution, skill level, demand and the number of sellable appointments available.

A profitable service menu begins with a defined client and capacity.

Use consultation, observation and controlled execution together. Think about the client's hair, head/face shape, routine, desired finish and maintenance. Make one deliberate decision at a time, then cross-check the result before making the next adjustment.

For CPD purposes, do more than remember a technique. Explain why it is suitable, recognise when it is not suitable, and be able to adapt it. That is the difference between copying a method and developing professional judgement.


**Professional evidence:** Professional application: using a real, recent or realistic case involving positioning, target client & service menu, identify the evidence you would gather, the main risk of making the wrong decision, the option you would choose, one credible alternative you would reject, and justify both decisions in at least 120 words.

### 9. Activity, reflection & final assessment

Professional CPD is strongest when knowledge changes what you do. Review the earlier stages and identify the decisions you will carry into real client work.

State what you changed, which measure should move and when you will review it.

Reflection should connect an action to a number.

Your final assessment checks understanding across the course. The reflection is your evidence of application: be specific about what you will change, how you will measure it and what further development you may need.
